EDUCATIONAL FIELD GUIDE

01 / OVERVIEW

Guarding the skies

The Royal Thai Air Force (RTAF) operates combat aircraft to help protect national airspace and support joint operations. Its fleet has evolved through several generations of aviation technology.

01 —

Air power with purpose

Fighter aircraft combine speed, sensors, communications, and trained crews. Their effectiveness depends on the entire system—not just the aircraft itself.

02 / AIRCRAFT

Meet the aircraft

A quick introduction to notable fighter types associated with the RTAF. Service status and fleet composition can change over time.

MULTIROLE

LOCKHEED MARTIN

F-16 Fighting Falcon

A single-engine multirole fighter known for its agility and broad range of mission options. The RTAF has operated F-16 variants for decades.

At a glance

Role: multirole fighter
Design: single engine, single seat in common configurations
Note: specific equipment varies by variant and upgrade.

MULTIROLE

SAAB

JAS 39 Gripen

A Swedish multirole fighter designed around modern sensors, data links, and flexible operations. Thailand operates Gripen C/D aircraft.

At a glance

Role: multirole fighter
Design: single-engine aircraft with advanced avionics
Note: capabilities depend on variant and configuration.

LIGHT FIGHTER

NORTHROP

F-5 Tiger II

A compact supersonic fighter with a long service history in Thailand. Upgraded F-5TH aircraft have received modernised systems.

At a glance

Role: light fighter / air defence
Design: twin-engine, compact airframe
Note: modernisation can extend the usefulness of older airframes.

Air power relies on people, maintenance, planning, and coordination. These building blocks help aircraft operate safely and effectively.
